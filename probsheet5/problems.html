3.1 Question 5 (Section A)

(Note: the numbers in this question are entirely made up and not based on any real homework / exam!)

  1. Suppose that events \(E_1\), \(E_2\) and \(E_3\) form a partition of a sample space \(\Omega\). State the law of total probability for calculating the probability of event \(F\) from its conditional probabilities given \(E_1\), \(E_2\) and \(E_3\). [1]

  2. Students who do at least 90% of the homework have a 90% chance of passing the exam. Students who do at most 60% of the homework have a 50% chance of passing the exam. We know that 20% of students do at least 90% of the homework, and 70% of students do more than 60% of the homework. Overall 70% of students pass the exam. What proportion of students who do between 60% and 90% of the homework pass the exam? Give your answer as a percentage. [3]

  3. What does it mean to say that three events \(E\), \(F\) and \(G\) are independent? [2]

3.2 Question 6 (Section A)

Some surprising election results have led to Parliament containing a proportion \(p\) of members from the Count Binface party, who are incapable of changing their minds about anything, and a proportion \((1-p)\) of members from the Monster Raving Loony party, who change their minds at random with probability \(r\) between successive votes on the same issue. (Every member of Parliament must vote “Yes” or “No” in each vote.)

In the course of a debate about the budget, there are three votes on the same issue.

  1. What is the probability that a randomly chosen member of Parliament voted in the same way on the first two votes? [2]

  2. Given that a randomly chosen member of Parliament voted in the same way on the first two votes, what is the probability that they will vote in the same way on the third vote? [2]

  3. A member of the Monster Raving Loony party initially decides to vote Yes with probability \(1/2\) and No with probability \(1/2\) on an issue. There is then another vote on the same issue. For what value of \(r\) is their second vote independent of their first vote? Justify your answer. [2]

3.3 Question 7 (Section B) (Hand in)

  1. For events \(E\) and \(F\) such that \(\mathbb{P}(F)>0\), state the definition of the conditional probability of \(E\) given \(F\). [1]

  2. What does it mean for events \(E_1\) and \(E_2\) to form a partition of a sample space \(\Omega\)? [1]

  3. A plane is equally likely to have crashed in one of two possible regions. If the plane crashed in region 1, the probability that a search of that region will successfully find the plane is judged to be \(9/10\). The corresponding figure for region 2 is \(4/5\). Let \(P_j\) be the event that the plane is in region \(j\), for \(j=1,2\). Let \(U_j\) be the event that a search of region \(j\) is unsuccessful, for \(j=1,2\). [7]

    (i) Explain why \(\mathbb{P}(U_2\, |\, P_1\cap U_1) = 1\).

    (ii) Calculate \(\mathbb{P}(P_1\cap U_1\cap U_2)\) and \(\mathbb{P}(P_2\cap U_1\cap U_2)\).

    (iii) Calculate the probability that the plane is in region 2, given that searches of both regions are unsuccessful. Write your answer as a fraction in its simplest form.

  1. A lake contains \(52\) fish of which \(13\) are perch and \(39\) are carp. Assume that carp and perch are equally likely to be caught. A fisherman has caught five fish. For this part of the question, you may leave your answers as fractions involving factorials or combinations. [6]

    (i) Find the probability that exactly two of the five caught fish are perch.

    (ii) You are told that the fisherman has caught at least three perch. What is the probability that all five of the caught fish are perch?
